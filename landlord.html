DP1 vs DP3 — Know the Difference

Many landlords end up with a DP1 (named-peril) policy because it's cheaper. But DP1 only covers a short list of named perils — if the cause of damage isn't explicitly listed, you're not covered.

A DP3 is open-peril: everything is covered unless specifically excluded. For rental properties, that's the coverage you actually need.

We write DP3 as the default, not the upgrade. Ask us about available enhancements including water backup, equipment breakdown, and extended replacement cost.

Common Questions

Landlord Insurance FAQ

Do I need landlord insurance if I already have a homeowners policy?

Yes. Standard homeowners (HO3) policies exclude rental activity. If you're renting to a tenant — even a family member — you need a landlord/dwelling fire policy. Renting with an HO3 in place is a claim-denial waiting to happen.

What's the difference between a DP1, DP2, and DP3?

DP1 is basic/named-peril (only the listed causes of loss are covered). DP2 is broad-form named-peril. DP3 is open-peril — everything is covered unless specifically excluded. We recommend DP3 for virtually all rental properties because of its comprehensive coverage scope.

Can I get coverage if the property is currently vacant?

Yes, though vacancy adds complexity. Most standard landlord policies have vacancy clauses that reduce or eliminate coverage after 30–60 days of vacancy. We can write standalone vacant property policies or add vacancy endorsements to bridge the gap between tenants.

Do you insure properties in both Georgia and Florida?

Yes. Pinnacle Risk Advisors is licensed in both Georgia and Florida. We have carrier access in both states including markets that specialize in coastal Florida property.

How fast can you quote and bind coverage?

Typically within 24 hours for standard SFR rental properties. For more complex risks (large portfolios, distressed properties, coastal Florida), allow 48–72 hours. Closings with short timelines — reach out directly and we'll make it work.

Should my tenant carry renter's insurance?

Yes — and we recommend requiring it in your lease. Your landlord policy covers the building structure and your liability. It does NOT cover your tenant's personal belongings. Requiring renter's insurance protects your tenant and reduces friction when losses occur.
