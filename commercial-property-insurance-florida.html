Coverage Options

Florida Commercial Property Coverage Options

The coverage options below are commonly considered for commercial property policies in Florida. Florida has unique market characteristics, and not all coverages are available from all carriers or in all areas. Availability depends on underwriting, property location, occupancy type, roof age, and the carrier selected.

Core Coverage

Building Coverage

Building coverage protects the physical structure of the commercial property from covered causes of loss. In Florida, coverage may be written on a replacement cost or actual cash value basis depending on underwriting, and may be subject to separate wind deductibles or wind exclusions depending on the carrier and property location. Accurate replacement cost valuation is particularly important in the Florida market.

Core Coverage

Business Personal Property (BPP)

Business personal property coverage can protect your furniture, equipment, inventory, and other business property inside the building from covered losses. In Florida, BPP coverage may also be subject to wind-related exclusions or deductibles depending on the carrier and policy structure. Tenants who do not own the building but have significant business property may also need BPP coverage.

Included Where Available

Business Income & Extra Expense

Business income coverage can be particularly valuable for Florida businesses given the potential for extended repair timelines following hurricane or wind events. If a covered loss forces you to suspend operations, this coverage may help replace lost income during the restoration period. In Florida, extended period of indemnity provisions may be especially relevant given the complexity of post-storm rebuilding.

Commonly Available

General Liability

General liability coverage protects your Florida business from third-party bodily injury and property damage claims. Many small businesses use a business owners policy (BOP) that bundles property and general liability coverage together. Separate commercial general liability policies are also available depending on the risk type and coverage structure needed.

Important Florida Consideration

Wind & Hurricane Coverage

Wind and hurricane coverage is one of the most critical and complex components of Florida commercial property insurance. Some carriers include wind as part of the policy; others exclude wind entirely or write wind on a limited basis. Coastal and high-wind zone properties often have more restricted options and may require coverage through specialty markets. Hurricane deductibles are often percentage-based rather than flat amounts. Understanding the wind coverage structure of any Florida policy is essential before binding.

Where Available

Equipment Breakdown

Equipment breakdown coverage may be available as an endorsement and can help cover losses from the sudden and accidental breakdown of covered equipment such as HVAC systems, electrical systems, and commercial equipment. In Florida, HVAC systems are particularly important given the year-round cooling demands, and standard property policies may not cover mechanical breakdown without a specific endorsement.

Important Florida Consideration

Ordinance or Law Coverage

Ordinance or law coverage may help cover the additional costs of rebuilding or repairing to current Florida Building Code standards after a covered loss. Florida’s building codes have been substantially updated following major hurricanes, and older structures may face significant code upgrade requirements during reconstruction. This coverage may be especially valuable for commercial properties built before Florida’s post-Andrew code revisions.

Where Available

Theft & Vandalism

Theft and vandalism coverage may be included in or available as part of a commercial property policy depending on the policy form and carrier. In Florida, post-hurricane periods can see elevated vandalism risk in affected areas. Coverage availability and limits vary by carrier and underwriting.

Endorsement

Water Damage Endorsements

Standard commercial property policies may exclude certain types of water damage such as sewer backup, sump overflow, or flood-related losses. In Florida, water intrusion associated with wind-driven rain events may be a coverage consideration that varies by policy and carrier. Water damage endorsements where available may be worth reviewing for Florida commercial properties, particularly those with basements or below-grade areas.

What Affects Pricing

What Affects Commercial Property Insurance Pricing in Florida

Florida commercial property insurance pricing is influenced by a range of factors that are unique to the state’s market, risk environment, and regulatory structure.

01

Wind Zone & Location

Florida properties are classified by wind zone based on their location and proximity to the coast. Properties in higher wind zones or coastal areas typically face higher premiums, more limited carrier options, and may have mandatory wind exclusions or higher deductibles.

02

Roof Age, Type & Construction

Roof age is one of the most significant underwriting factors in Florida commercial property. Many carriers apply depreciation schedules for older roofs or may decline replacement cost coverage beyond a certain roof age. Roof shape (hip vs. gable) and construction can also affect pricing.

03

Year Built & Building Code Compliance

Buildings constructed to post-Andrew or more recent Florida Building Code standards often receive more favorable underwriting treatment. Older construction or structures built to superseded standards may face higher rates or more limited options.

04

Occupancy Type & Risk

The type of business operating in the property affects underwriting. High-hazard occupancies such as restaurants, certain manufacturing, or chemical storage operations carry different risk profiles than professional offices or retail stores.

05

Wind Mitigation Features

Commercial properties with qualifying wind mitigation features may qualify for premium credits. A wind mitigation inspection can document qualifying features and may result in significant savings for Florida commercial property policies.

06

Distance to Coast & Flood Zone

Proximity to the coast affects wind exposure, carrier availability, and pricing. Flood zone designation also affects coverage options, particularly for properties in FEMA Special Flood Hazard Areas, though flood is typically a separate coverage consideration.

07

Replacement Cost & Coverage Amount

Accurate building valuation is particularly important in Florida where construction costs can be elevated post-disaster. Underinsuring to save on premium can result in significant out-of-pocket exposure if a major loss occurs.

08

Prior Loss History

A history of claims can affect pricing and carrier appetite. In Florida, wind-related losses in prior years are particularly scrutinized during underwriting, especially if there are multiple events or significant severity.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Commercial Property Insurance Mistakes to Avoid in Florida

Florida’s commercial property insurance market is among the most complex in the country. These are common mistakes that can leave Florida businesses and property owners with significant coverage gaps.

Not Understanding the Wind Coverage Structure Before Binding

Wind coverage in Florida can be structured in multiple ways: as part of the policy, excluded from the policy, or covered under a separate wind policy. The deductible structure can also vary dramatically (flat vs. percentage-based). Binding without understanding exactly what your wind coverage looks like is a common and potentially costly mistake.

Waiting Until Hurricane Season to Review Coverage

Once a named storm is being tracked, most carriers suspend binding operations. If you have a coverage gap heading into hurricane season, it may be too late to address it after a storm forms. Reviewing and addressing coverage gaps before the season is a better approach.

Undervaluing the Building Replacement Cost

Post-disaster construction costs in Florida can be significantly elevated due to material shortages and high contractor demand. Setting building coverage below current replacement cost to reduce premium can result in a coinsurance shortfall when you need coverage most. Accurate valuation is particularly important in Florida.

Skipping Business Income Coverage

Florida businesses are particularly vulnerable to extended business interruption from hurricane damage, especially for properties that require substantial structural repair. Business income coverage may be one of the most valuable components of a Florida commercial property program, yet it is sometimes omitted to reduce premium.

Not Getting a Wind Mitigation Inspection

A wind mitigation inspection can document qualifying features of your Florida commercial property and potentially qualify you for meaningful premium credits. If your property has qualifying roof coverings, deck attachments, or opening protections, not having an inspection means you may be paying more than necessary.

Not Reviewing Ordinance or Law Exposure

Florida’s building codes have been significantly updated following major hurricanes. If your commercial building was constructed before current codes, a covered loss may trigger code upgrade requirements that substantially exceed the direct damage costs. Ordinance or law coverage may be worth reviewing for older Florida commercial structures.

Frequently Asked Questions

Florida Commercial Property Insurance FAQ

What does commercial property insurance cover in Florida?
Commercial property insurance for Florida businesses commonly covers the building structure, business personal property, and may include business income coverage. Florida-specific considerations include wind and hurricane coverage structure, which can vary significantly by carrier and location. Carriers may include wind in the policy, exclude wind, or require a separate wind policy depending on the property location and underwriting.
Is wind and hurricane damage covered under Florida commercial property insurance?
Wind and hurricane coverage in Florida varies significantly by carrier, program, and property location. Some carriers include wind as part of the policy; others exclude wind entirely or write it on a restricted basis. Coastal properties and those in higher wind zones may have more limited options or may need to obtain wind coverage through specialty markets. Hurricane deductibles in Florida are often percentage-based rather than flat amounts, which can result in significant out-of-pocket exposure in a major event.
What is ordinance or law coverage and why does it matter for Florida commercial properties?
Ordinance or law coverage may help cover the additional costs of rebuilding or repairing to current Florida Building Code standards after a covered loss. Florida’s building codes have been substantially updated since major hurricane events, and older commercial structures may face significant mandatory code upgrades during reconstruction. Without this coverage, the cost of code compliance could come entirely out of pocket even if the property damage itself is covered.
How does the hurricane deductible work on Florida commercial property policies?
Many Florida commercial property policies include a separate hurricane deductible expressed as a percentage of the insured building value rather than a flat dollar amount. For example, a 5% hurricane deductible on a $500,000 building means $25,000 out of pocket before hurricane coverage applies. Understanding the deductible structure is a critical part of reviewing any Florida commercial property policy.
Is business income coverage important for Florida commercial businesses?
Business income coverage can be particularly valuable for Florida businesses given the potential for hurricane-related property damage requiring extended repair timelines. If a covered loss forces your Florida business to suspend operations for weeks or months, this coverage may help replace lost income during the restoration period. For businesses with significant overhead costs or revenue concentration, business income coverage is worth reviewing carefully.

Florida carrier availability varies significantly by location, occupancy type, and property characteristics. We shop multiple markets to find the best available fit for your Florida commercial property.
